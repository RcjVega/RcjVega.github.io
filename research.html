Research Agenda

Understanding how infrastructure and institutions shape development

My research examines how infrastructure investment, access to public services, natural resources and regulatory institutions affect poverty, welfare and regional economic performance. I combine applied microeconomics with panel-data methods and quasi-experimental strategies, with a primary focus on Peru and Latin America.

Research Field I

Infrastructure Economics and Poverty

This research line studies how investment in energy, telecommunications, transport, water and sanitation infrastructure affects total and extreme poverty. My work distinguishes aggregate and sectoral investment effects and examines differences between public and private investment.

Sectoral Effects

How do poverty effects differ across energy, telecommunications, transport, water and sanitation infrastructure?

Investment Composition

Do public and private infrastructure investments generate different socioeconomic outcomes?

Dynamic Effects

How persistent are poverty dynamics and how quickly do infrastructure effects materialize across regions?

Research Field II

Impact Evaluation and Local Development

I am interested in identifying the causal effects of public-service expansion and local economic shocks. Current research themes include water and sanitation access, human-capital outcomes, mining activity, poverty and heterogeneous regional development effects.

Research Field III

Regulation and Competition Policy

This line examines how regulatory institutions, market structure and firm incentives shape economic outcomes. My interests include economic regulation, market power, strategic interaction, mergers, innovation incentives, patents and evidence-based competition policy.
