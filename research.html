Research Agenda

My research examines how infrastructure investment, market organization and regulatory institutions influence poverty, welfare and economic performance. I use applied econometric methods and policy analysis, with a primary focus on Peru and Latin America.

Infrastructure Economics and Poverty

This research line studies the effects of investment in energy, telecommunications, transport, water and sanitation infrastructure on total and extreme poverty. Current work uses regional panel data to distinguish aggregate, sectoral, public and private investment effects.

Core questions

  • How large are the poverty effects of infrastructure investment?
  • Do effects differ across infrastructure sectors?
  • Are public and private investments associated with different outcomes?
  • How persistent are poverty dynamics across regions?

Industrial Organization and Competition Policy

My interests include market power, oligopoly models, strategic interaction, horizontal mergers, innovation incentives, patents and the design of competition policy.

Transport Economics and Regulation

This line focuses on economic regulation in urban public transport, including regulatory accounting, cost allocation, financial information, operator incentives and evidence-based regulatory decisions.

Applied Econometrics

Methods used in my research include pooled models, fixed effects, random effects, dynamic panel models, Arellano–Bond estimators, GMM, panel causality tests, cross-sectional dependence tests and panel unit-root analysis.
